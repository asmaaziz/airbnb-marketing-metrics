What Airbnb's marketing spend actually buys

Airbnb reports revenue, bookings and total sales & marketing spend. It doesn't report what that spend buys. This page derives the missing efficiency metrics from its annual filings.

Marginal cost of a new night

Formula: Δ Sales & marketing ÷ Δ Nights booked, year over year

Bookings bought per marketing dollar

Formula: Gross booking value ÷ Sales & marketing

Marketing's share of revenue vs. the take rate

Formulas: Sales & marketing ÷ Revenue and Revenue ÷ Gross booking value

Price vs. volume

Formula: Average daily rate ≈ Gross booking value ÷ Nights booked. Growth split into price and volume.

Is the next marketing dollar still paying back?

Incremental return: Δ Revenue ÷ Δ Marketing spend, year over year. Shown for total sales & marketing and for ad spend only.

Where does the marketing budget actually go?

Split of sales & marketing into ad spend (brand and performance marketing) and everything else (field operations, policy, staff).

The data

Sources. Every figure comes from Airbnb's own filings, linked in the table's Source column. 2019–2020 revenue and marketing are from the FY2020 10-K and nights and bookings from the FY2021 10-K; 2021–2022 from the FY2022 10-K; 2023–2024 from the FY2024 10-K; 2025 from the FY2025 10-K and Q4 2025 shareholder letter. All filings: SEC EDGAR.

2020 caution. 2020 sales & marketing includes about $435M of one-time stock-based compensation recognized at the December 2020 IPO, so 2020 marketing costs look higher than the actual ad spend that year.

Limits. Sales & marketing includes brand, performance and some staff costs, and Airbnb doesn't split them publicly. Year-over-year deltas treat all new bookings as marketing-driven, which overstates marketing's role; read the marginal metric as a ceiling, not a precise CAC. Nights include experiences.
